Welcome to the Questions ‘n’ Answers page of the Wiki. Here you will find a collection of questions routinely posed to the CK3 Discord server as well as their corresponding answers given by members of the community. Information given will assume the default setup, such as a Greek character being the Byzantine Emperor at game start. Every game is different and could result in varying conditions, but this is a guide with which to begin.

### Why can’t I declare war against the Byzantine Empire if I have a claimant in my court?

The Byzantine Empire has a built-in protection system in the form of the Byzantine Traditions cultural tradition. With it, any character with any negative physical traits cannot have their claims pressed against them. Another factor to consider is if your courtier is female, as the Byzantine Empire (and most realms in general) follow Male Preference Succession law. This means that women’s claims cannot be pressed against men but can be pressed against other women and children.

### Why aren’t most members of my family getting the Born in the Purple trait if I’m playing the Byzantine Empire?

The Born in the Purple trait is only given to direct children of the Emperor. Grandchildren and nieces/nephews are disqualified. When playing as the Byzantine Empire, a good rule on succession is never let your children marry until succession happens, then marry off your siblings to grow your dynasty and get alliances if you need them. This way, you can always be assured that the Born in the Purple trait will occur for all of your rulers.

### How does succession function in the first place?

Think of a vertical line on your screen or paper. That line represents your family. Any children you have after your first child, as well as children that your siblings have, represent the line going in various directions, deviating away from the original vertical line. Succession prefers to go straight down the line as much as possible. Your oldest son stands to inherit, then his eldest son, and so on. If a son is not available, then the eldest daughter stands to inherit. If you don’t have any children at all, then succession goes horizontal from you and tries to find your eldest brother, then eldest sister. If you have no siblings, then succession goes reverse vertically back up to your parents, then uncles, then aunts. Remember that finding the next person to inherit always resets upon a new ruler being crowned. It could be the case that distant cousins will stand to inherit too!

### What should I do with all the Prestige that I’ve accumulated throughout my ruler’s life?

The fact that you’ve lived a long enough life and gained quite a lot of Prestige is a good feat. There are a couple of options for you to consider. The first is any kind of cultural reformation. You can add in or swap cultural traditions to your culture, each one with varying amounts of Prestige; usually they will cost around 5000 Prestige each. If you have even more than that, you might even consider reforming your culture’s Ethos, which will cost you 20000 Prestige. Finally, another option to consider for your Prestige will be putting electives on your titles to help you manage your succession in the early and mid-game.

### What can I do to best handle succession in the early and mid-game before my culture unlocks Primogeniture during the late-game?

This is where electives come in quite handy. If you don’t want to spend Renown on disinheriting your children, or don’t want to spend Legitimacy on sending your children off to become monks or holy orders members, or you don’t want to be a tyrant and imprison them unjustly…or worse yet, let them rot in your dungeon to die, execute them yourself, or let them be knights and let them die in battle, then electives are your solution. There are various electives in the game, ie: Tanistry, Saxon, Scandinavian, Feudal, and Tribal. Each one has various rules about candidates, electors, and voting power. The one thing they all do the same is help to ensure a single heir. If you have multiple duchies, you will need to put the elective on all of them. If you add a kingdom, you need to put an elective on that kingdom title too. If you have multiple kingdom titles, then all of them need the elective and you are free to remove them from the duchies. If you have an empire, it will need an elective. If you have multiple empires, they all will need them and you can remove all electives from kingdoms. If you hold any titles outside of your de jure primary title, they will need electives too. All electives allow your children to be candidates. You will always be an elector. Be a good ruler to get votes from other electors because they like you - or be a tyrannical ruler and use Dread or Hooks to force votes to go your way. This is by far the best way to handle partition succession in the game.

### Why does my vassal dislike me if I’m not their rightful liege?

The rightful liege penalty is a small way that the game helps you to determine how to set up your realm in terms of vassal management. Counts can be vassals to dukes, and dukes can be vassals to kings and emperors without penalty. For example, the Byzantine Emperor can have the King of Georgia as a vassal. They can also have the Duke of the Aegean Islands as a vassal. But they shouldn’t have the Count of Rama as a vassal. In this case, you should make the Duchy of Upper Bosnia and give the count that title. Another effect of the rightful liege penalty is earning reduced taxes and levies from your vassals if your realm isn’t set up correctly. Giving rulers more lands helps stabilize things too. You can have more rulers helping you to Increase Control, Convert Faith, and in some cases to help you Promote Culture in counties not of your culture too.

### What do I have to do to get my vassals to convert culture in their counties?

The debate is still on-going is to what helps in this process, but there are a couple of things that do help. Vassals are very much included to keep their Stewards on the Collect Taxes task, but they will help you out sometimes. Before you begin, try to only have count level vassals help you; dukes are far too interested in warfare. First, the county you want converted must border a county of the desired culture. Second, the new culture has to share a heritage with the desired culture. Third, the desired culture gets a buff to convert lands of the old culture if it’s a new hybrid or divergent culture. Fourth, if the desired culture has more innovations or higher era level, vassals might convert their lands too. Ultimately, culture is meant to be more rooted to counties and therefore the game does have protections in place to ensure that the whole world doesn’t convert to a single culture very easily, unlike Faith. Very few decisions in the game can help with culture conversion, like Outremer.

### How did the Pope unlock Crusades before the year 1096?

The game is giving us historical fiction, and part of that is seeing how the Pope would handle affairs prior to the First Crusade. How it does this is by monitoring the status of the Catholic holy sites; Jerusalem, Rome, Cologne, Santiago, and Canterbury/Kent. The Pope is programmed to be a little more lenient if other faiths hold Jerusalem, but if the other four sites fall to a faith outside Catholicism, the Pope gets upset. The Pope himself controls Rome, so it’s mostly just the other three sites that need to be considered. Most often you will find that Islamic or heretical Christian rulers have taken Santiago or that Asatru or heretical Christian rulers have taken Cologne or Canterbury/Kent. The likelihood of the Pope unlocking crusades dramatically increases if any of these should happen. Similarly, if you grant one of these counties to a non-Catholic and grant them independence, you can intentionally help the Pope unlock crusades too, which is needed for an Outremer Empire game.

### How do I win a crusade that I’m pledged to fight with?

The warfare that takes place during crusades is unlike any other warfare in the game. The Pope is the war leader, and all his allies will flock to his banners. Capturing war leaders means no change in war score. What you want to do is participate as much as you can, since participation is what determines the rewards given out if you can win. To do this, standard sieges and battles count. The best part is that there is no cap on sieges and battles. Normally, winning battles caps the war score to 50% - during crusades this doesn’t happen. Protect your army as much as possible. Attrition will not be your friend. In rare circumstances, you might even have to send your army to fight a losing battle - this is completely OK too since you still have earned a higher participation score. Keep your army close to the Pope’s and help him by sieging close around him. Then split up your armies and resupply on your sieged land. It will be a long and hard fought fight, but memorable for the rest of your game.

### Why can’t I raid anymore?

The ability to raid is given only to rulers who are Tribal or have an Unreformed Faith. If you are Feudal/Clan and have an Organized Faith, you cannot raid anymore unless you have the Practiced Pirates cultural tradition or have the Legacy of Piracy dynasty modifier from Elevating the Kingdom of Mann & and the Isles.

### How do I switch to Feudal from Clan government and vice versa?

There are two ways to accomplish this. First, you must conquer a tribal county. Second, give away all your feudalized counties, including your capital after you have moved it to the tribal county. You will then have access to the Decision to Embrace Tribalism. Then you can Adopt Feudal or Clan Ways as per the usual. You will be able to Adopt Clan Ways if you follow an Islamic faith and Feudal for every other faith. The next way to switch government type is to have your heir landed as the desired government type. For example, if you are a Clan Emperor and your heir is a Feudal Count, upon succession you will be a Feudal Emperor.

### What accolades should I use?

The best accolades in the game are Retinue Accolades. These allow for the recruitment of Retinue Men-at-Arms to a hard cap of 6, and better yet, increase the regiment size of non-retinue Men-at-Arms and increase their overall stats as well. Retinue Men-at-Arms are a great way to bypass cultural limitations to get early access to units that you normally shouldn’t be able to have, such as Armoured Horsemen during the Tribal era. The accolade’s effects are determined by the knight’s culture, not your own.

### How do I install mods?

To install mods on Steam, either click the link to the mod you have found, or type in the name of the mod into the Steam workshop. Then subscribe to the mod. Next, launch the game but don’t click Resume or Play. Go to Installed Mods and make sure it shows up. You may also be inclined to set up different playsets too so that you can play different modded versions of the game, or revert back to vanilla. Your game, your choice, your destiny!
