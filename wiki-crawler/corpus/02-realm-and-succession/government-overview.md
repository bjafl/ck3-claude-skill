A government determines which laws a character can use and what obligations a vassal owes their liege. Governments only change via decisions. There are three families: **landed**, **domicile**, and **non-dynastic**. See dedicated files for [Feudal](government-feudal.md), [Tribal](government-tribal.md), [Clan](government-clan.md), [Nomadic](government-nomadic.md), and [Administrative](government-administrative.md); the remaining types below have no separate wiki page and are documented here only.

## Landed governments

Require the character to own landed titles. If a player loses all landed titles they must switch dynasty member or (with Roads to Power) become Adventurer. Inheriting a higher-rank realm converts the heir to that realm's government.

| Government | Required DLC |
|---|---|
| Clan | — |
| Feudal | — |
| Mandala | All Under Heaven |
| Tribal | — |
| Wanua | All Under Heaven |

- **Mandala**: no dedicated wiki page found. Converting to a Monotheist-doctrine faith turns a Mandala ruler Feudal; being vassalized by a liege of a different government also converts them to the liege's government.
- **Wanua**: no dedicated wiki page found; without All Under Heaven, replaced by Tribal.

## Domicile governments

Give house heads (or all rulers) a domicile, letting the house persist without landed titles. Switching to a landed government sells the domicile for gold, removes all title men-at-arms, and converts Treasury/Herd resources to gold. Inheriting a higher-rank realm does **not** change a domicile government.

| Government | Domicile | Required DLC |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative | Administrative estate | Roads to Power |
| Adventurer | Camp | Roads to Power |
| Celestial | Celestial estate | All Under Heaven |
| Meritocratic | Celestial estate | All Under Heaven |
| Meritocratic Khanate | Celestial estate | All Under Heaven |
| Nomadic | Nomadic capital | Khans of the Steppe |
| Ritsuryō | Manor | All Under Heaven |
| Sōryō | Manor | All Under Heaven |

- **Adventurer**: landless playstyle unlocked by Roads to Power; a character with no titles chooses Adventurer instead of a game over.
- **Celestial / Meritocratic / Meritocratic Khanate / Ritsuryō / Sōryō**: no dedicated wiki pages found — East/South Asian government variants added by All Under Heaven. _Not covered in detail on the wiki._

## Non-dynastic governments

Not playable; no dynastic succession. On death, a random character inherits all titles. Inheriting a higher-rank realm does not change government.

| Government | Requirements | Tithe as vassal |
|---|---|---|
| Herder | Khans of the Steppe | Other rulers cannot use the Demand Pastureland interaction |
| Holy Order | Holy Order title | Armies can be hired for free |
| Mercenary Company | Mercenary title | No tithe |
| Republic | City holding as realm capital | 20% Taxes, 10% Levies |
| Theocracy | Theocratic Clerical Tradition doctrine + Temple holding as realm capital | 10% Taxes/Levies if different faith; if same faith, taxes/levies scale with the liege's devotion |

- **Herder**: tends flocks only; if the player selects a Herder character, government auto-converts to Nomadic at game start. Without Khans of the Steppe, replaced by Tribal.
- **Holy Order**: holy warriors garrisoning donated castles; leader chosen from within the order.
- **Mercenary Company**: hired-army roamers; leader chosen from within the company.
- **Republic**: mayor chosen by burgher-class popularity.
- **Theocracy**: ruled by a priest/priestess. Realm Priests pass 25% of vassal income up from their liege's direct vassals, and up to 50% of income from directly-controlled churches and vassal priests to their feudal liege (opinion-scaled).
