A title is essentially a certificate of land ownership, decreeing which characters own (or have a right to own) a certain place or lead a certain landless group of people. To "hold title" to a piece of land makes the titleholder "entitled" to that land, which is where modern land ownership and property law is descended from. Each title has a rank, a unique coat of arms, and a color on map. A character's titles along with those held by its vassals form a character's Realm.

Certain titles (and the holders Form of address) can change name based on the culture of the character holding it or their government type, and all titles can have their names and coat of arms changed by their holder. Some titles will change also their coat of arms automatically based on the faith of their holder but can be manually reverted to the historical coat of arms at any time.

A character's highest-ranked title is the Primary Title and determines the realm's color and coat of arms. If a character owns multiple titles of the same highest rank, they can freely choose which one is the Primary title.

If a character is granted a title all lower-ranked vassals whose realms are located entirely within the de jure realm of the granted title will be transferred.

If you own a title, you can edit it's Coat of arms.

## Title rank

Each title has a rank, which determines its place in the feudal vassal hierarchy. Rank primarily governs who (in possession of what titles) holders of that title can vassalize or become vassals of. A ruler can only have vassals of a lower title rank and can only be a vassal of someone of a higher title rank. Title Rank also determines what bonuses it grants to players who hold that title. Creating a title requires owning at least two titles of lower rank or one title of equal or higher rank and vassal cannot create titles of equal rank to their liege's. The lowest playable title is Count, Barons are not playable.

All opinion modifiers from granting titles last 50 years and can stack. Creation costs can be reduced by various modifiers up to a 20% minimum cost. (So creating a Duchy will always cost at least 50 gold).

### Duchy

Duchy is the third rank of titles, and the first rank that can be destroyed or created by characters. However, it has some unique properties:

- Each duchy has a duchy capital building slot in its capital county's capital barony.
- Characters can hold at most 2 duchy titles without suffering opinion penalties from their vassals; this rule does not apply to any other ranks.
- Emperors and Hegemons can have duchy rank vassals without penalties.

## De Jure

De Jure, meaning "by law" (compare and contrast with De facto, which means "by fact", or how things actually are), comes up when looking at title composition. Each title above baronies is de jure comprised of multiple smaller lower-ranking titles, and is itself de jure a constituent part of a larger higher-ranking title.

Every duchy in the game is considered to be part of a specific de jure kingdom and every kingdom to be part of a specific de jure empire. Vassals within a de jure border of a title held by their liege have fewer reasons and abilities to revolt against their liege and declare independence.

The Duchy, Kingdom and Empire map modes will show all De Jure titles.

A character who owns a title can choose the De Jure Casus belli to seize a single De Jure title covered by it, provided that the character's culture has the appropriate innovation below:

- Seize De Jure County requires the Tribal-era innovation Casus Belli
- Seize De Jure Duchy requires the Early Medieval-era innovation Chronicle Writing
- Seize De Jure Titles requires the Late Medieval-era innovation Rightful Ownership

If a vassal's primary title does not fall under the de jure territory of any titles their liege holds, that vassal is considered to be de facto outside of its de jure territory and gives -50% less Taxes and Levies alongside -5 Opinion of Liege. Furthermore, count rank vassals give -25% less Taxes and Levies alongside -5 Opinion of Liege if their liege does not hold the de jure duchy of their primary title, but does hold its de jure kingdom or empire.

Any ducal or kingdom title can be created by a character who controls more than 50% of its De Jure Counties as long as they hold either two titles of lower Rank or one title of equal or higher Rank; empire titles require controlling over 80% of their De Jure Counties. Legitimacy above expectations provides a discount to the required amount of counties.

In the Region of Europe, AI characters will only create Kingdom titles if they have certain cultures.

### Usurpation

If a character has at least two titles of lower rank or one title of equal or higher rank and is holding more than 50% of the De Jure Counties of a title held by a Ruler of the same Faith or a Faith which is not considered Hostile or Evil, then the character can Usurp the title. This process gives the title to the usurper. If the holder's Faith is considered Hostile or Evil, then they must not hold any De Jure counties of the title to be able to be Usurped. Titles cannot be usurped from war enemies.

If a character is a Vassal, they cannot Usurp a title belonging to the Liege, nor can they Usurp one that has the same Rank or higher than the Liege's Primary title.

Usurping a title will give -50 Opinion with the title's previous owner and they will lose Legitimacy.

### De jure drift

De jure drift or assimilation is the process by which the de jure borders of kingdoms, empires and hegemonies can change over time, effectively changing which title is part of which other title or what smaller titles comprise a larger title.

If a ruler completely controls a duchy, kingdom or empire and owns a higher ranked title as their Primary Title that the lower title does not De Jure belong to but has a land connection to or is within 2 sea tiles of it, then the said duchy, kingdom or empire will slowly become De Jure part of the character's Primary title as long as it remains under that ruler's control.

A duchy will begin to drift into a kingdom when all of the following conditions are met:

- The entire duchy (i.e. all county titles belonging de jure to the duchy; baronies are allowed to be outside the realm) is within the realm of the king.
- The duchy is not part of the kingdom of Jerusalem.
- The kingdom is its owner's primary title. (Note: there are some corner cases / possible bugs where drift continues even if primary title changes)
- The duchy either shares a land border with, or is a maximum of 2 sea tiles away from the existing de jure kingdom.
- The de jure kingdom of the duchy is not drifting into another empire which is not the de jure empire of the kingdom
- Note: it does not matter if the duchy title actually exists or not. If it is destroyed, it will still experience de jure drift and when recreated, it will be part of the title it has drifted to by that point.

A kingdom will begin to drift into an empire when all of the following conditions are met:

- The entire kingdom (i.e. all county titles of all duchy titles belonging de jure to the kingdom title) is within the realm of the emperor.
- The empire is its owner's primary title.
- The kingdom either shares a land border with, or is a maximum of 2 sea tiles away from the existing de jure empire.

An empire will begin to drift into a hegemony when all of the following conditions are met:

- The entire empire (i.e. all county titles of all kingdom titles belonging de jure to the empire title ) is within the realm of the hegemon.
- The hegemony is its owner's primary title.
- The empire either shares a land border with, or is a maximum of 2 sea tiles away from the existing de jure hegemony.

Upon reaching January 1st of the next year, drift is visible as dashed stripes on the kingdoms, empires or hegemonies map mode. Hovering over the duchy or kingdom will show a tooltip indicating the number of years until full assimilation.

Whenever the conditions for assimilation are no longer met — for example, if the local ruler of a duchy rebels against the top-most liege, or if one of the counties in the duchy is conquered by another independent realm — the counter will tick backwards instead of forwards, at half the speed. When the conditions are again met — for example, if the duke's rebellion is suppressed or the title is reconquered by its previous realm — the counter will resume ticking forwards.

Drift progress is recorded towards each target separately. If drift target changes, progress starts accumulating towards the new target at the same time as it ticks back towards the old target.

In general, a title will be assimilated after being controlled for 100 years. The duration required for assimilation can only be decreased by the Chancellor task "Integrate Title".

Completing a drift requires accumulating 1200 progress. You can see current numeric progress using the "Integrate Title" task.

Assimilation allows titular kingdoms and empires to become non-titular if they assimilate a landed title, and vice versa if a landed title's constituent titles are all assimilated by another title. If a de jure kingdom becomes titular, it can be created by whoever controls its de jure capital, with no requirement for controlling its de jure duchies.

## Claim

A claim represents a character's legal right to obtain a title which is not theirs and allows the creation of a claimant faction. If the title belongs to a vassal, the claim allows the liege to revoke the claimed title without incurring tyranny. Otherwise, a claim provides a casus belli to gain the claimed title through war. Alternatively, if the title belongs to the liege and the government is tribal, the claimant vassal can use the ‘Challenge the Ruler’ decision, which is a duel. Claims can be pressed, unpressed, or implicit.

- Pressed claims on a character's titles are given to all legitimate children upon the title holder's death, except to primary heirs under any partition succession. Primary heirs under a partition succession do not inherit claims on their siblings land, with the exception of any claims for titles that are of equal rank to their primary title. This allows primary heirs under a partition succession to quickly declare war to reunite their predecessor's realm. Also, pressed claims held by the decedent are inherited by legitimate children as unpressed claims regardless of sex. Wars over pressed claims cost 25% less.
- Unpressed claims are never inherited but can become pressed if a claim war using the claim ends in a white peace.
- Implicit claims on a character's current titles are given to children favored by the faith's View on Gender doctrine while the title holder is still alive. Children with traits that prevent inheriting titles will not get claims.

Claims on landed titles can be obtained the following ways:

- A vassal that owns over 50% of the counties from one of their liege's titles can use Claim Liege Title interaction to get an unpressed claim on that title at the cost of 1500 prestige.
- Using the Fabricate Claim on County councilor task will provide an unpressed claim on the targeted county. If the holder of the targeted county also holds its de jure duchy title and the Religious affairs councilor has high learning, there is a 20% chance of gaining a claim on the county's de jure duchy instead.
- A dynasty head can use the claim title interaction on dynasty members of the same religion to gain an unpressed claim on one of their titles.
- A character with the Sanctioned Loopholes perk can use the Buy Claim interaction to gain an unpressed claim on any one of another character's held titles for a fee in Gold.
- A character with a spiritual head of faith may use the Request Claim interaction to request an unpressed claim on one of a co-religionist's currently held titles for a Piety cost.

## Revocation

Revoking titles is where a liege strips a vassal's title from its holder and it is a crucial part of realm management, as it allows redistribution of power. Revoking titles requires the realm's authority law to be at least at level 2.

Revoking Titles incurs +20 Tyranny unless the liege has a Claim on the title being revoked, the vassal committed a Crime that allows or warrants revocation or the title is a barony.

If the vassal doesn't accept the revocation, they will rise in revolt, along with other disgruntled vassals. Barons and imprisoned vassals cannot refuse title revocation.

## Holding too many titles

Overextension: If a character is a Count or Duke, their realm can include a maximum of 30 County titles without penalties; for Dukes, this include the Counties controlled by their Count Vassals. Each county over the limit gives an overextension penalty that reduces the character's Gold income by -5%. Count-rank characters are unlikely to see this however, as they are further limited by their Domain Limit, which is usually much lower.

Too Many Held Duchies: If a character is a King or above, they can hold a maximum of 2 Duchy titles without penalties. Any Duchy above the limit reduces the Opinion of all Vassals by -15.

## Head of faith titles

Head of faith titles are not part of the de jure hierarchy as they do not contain any de jure territory. Instead they designate which character is a the head of faith for faiths with Spiritual or Temporal head of faith doctrine. Creating a head of faith title requires control of a Holy Site, 300 Gold and at least a Devoted level of devotion. All head of faith titles have Duke rank, with the exception of the Catholicism and Orthodoxy ones which have Kingdom rank.

## Roman Empire

The Roman Empire is an initially titular Hegemony title that has special mechanics. It can be created via the Restore the Roman Empire decision and will replace the current primary title. If multiple empire titles are held, they will all be merged into the Roman Empire title which will gain all their de jure territory. The character holding the Roman Empire title will always have the Augustus trait. The initial coat-of-arms of the title depends on which of the 3 required Empire titles the character taking the decision had as their primary title (which are, the Holy Roman Empire, Byzantine Empire or Empire of Italia). Characters holding the Roman Empire title will have access to the Restore Imperial Province casus belli.

If the [Roads to Power] Roads to Power DLC is not installed, rulers of the Roman Empire or Byzantine Empire who follow a Christian or Greco-Roman faith will gain +350 Prestige whenever they gain control of all counties from the duchies within one of the empire's old provinces as well as provinces it originally failed to conquer. Restoring all provinces will grant an additional +350 Prestige.

## Empire of China

The Empire of China is a unique Hegemony title covering the region of China and the only one that exists at the start of the game in 867, 1066 and 1178.

The Hegemony of China comprises 5 Empire titles (Jingyang, Liangyi, Lingnan, Yongliang, Zhongyuan), 30 Kingdom titles and well over 100 Duchy titles within its de jure territory. It uses the Celestial government type and is always a hereditary title passed on to the eldest living male heir of the ruler. The vassal titles within the Hegemony of China are not hereditary, however, and are instead awarded through appointment by the titleholder of the Hegemony of China, with Merit being the primary deciding factor in who is appointed.

The Hegemony of China is subject to the unique Dynastic Cycle situation, which progresses through different phases or eras throughout the game, with the actions of the Emperor and all of his numerous vassal rulers influencing which direction the cycle, and ultimately China, goes in. There are five different phases in the cycle, those being Advancement, Expansion, Tension, Conquest and Division. Each era changes how things work and what is and is not possible within the vast bureaucratic administration of the realm, enabling or disabling different laws and policies, casus bellis, interactions, cicuit (vassal) types and great projects, but the most radical of these is the Division phase. If the Dynastic Cycle progresses into the Division phase, then the Hegemony of China title and all of its constituent titles down to the Kingdom level are instantly destroyed (with the only exception being titles whose holders have sufficient Movement Power), and China effectively shatters into a chaotic mess of warlords and local rulers vying for power as all of the former subjects of the hegemon (who suffers an immediate succession) become independent. All independent rulers in China gain the Consolidate Land Under Heaven casus belli that they can use against each other to conquer land.

To restore the Hegemony of China title and end the Division era, an independent ruler must control over 50% of all de jure counties within the Hegemony of China, which allows them to claim the Mandate of Heaven, recreating the Hegemony of China title and awarding it to the claimant as their primary title. All rulers who hold titles that are de jure part of the Hegemony of China become vassals of the new Son of Heaven. The empire and kingdom titles that were destroyed when China shattered can now be recreated and given to appointed vassals by the new Emperor of China. The new Emperor of China is given a choice of whether to advance the Dynastic Cycle to the Advancement or Expansion phases and is given the opportunity to choose a name and preferred color for their dynasty, which will affect what name and color will be shown on the map and UI for the Hegemony of China.

The Hegemony of China is named Tang in 867, and Song in 1066 and 1178. Historically, it would be named Yuan from 1271 to 1368 and Ming from 1368 to 1644, the latter of which is past Crusader Kings 3's end date in 1453.
