Administrative is the Byzantine Empire's government (only the Byzantine Empire starts with it, regardless of start date; other realms can via the Extra Administrative Realms game rule). Without Roads to Power, it's replaced by Feudal. It is landless: house heads get an Administrative estate domicile. A ruler can only hold Administrative government as a King+ or as a vassal of an Administrative liege; otherwise it reverts to Feudal.

Grants access to Administrative Authority/Administrative laws, the Treasury resource, the Eparch court position, a distinct succession law, and the Bureaucracy legacy track for Dynasty Heads. Administrative vassals don't use Legitimacy. Regiment limit is −2 and Levies −50%, but rulers can raise 3–6 title men-at-arms per owned title (scales with rank), inherited with the title and effectiveness-scaled by the owner's Governor Efficiency.

Has a free-to-convert-to State Faith; characters convert to it more readily and away from it more reluctantly. The top liege can change the State Faith to their own for −5000 Piety/Influence and −30 opinion with Zealot vassals. Families whose house head doesn't follow the State Faith get −20 Powerful Family Rating.

Feudal and Clan rulers can adopt Administrative by decision or liege request. It also unlocks the Inspection activity and the Develop Communities great project.

## Bureaucracy (centralized bureaucracy government)

Unlocks the Influence resource, Political schemes, the Bribe Officials countermeasure focus, holding/cadet-branch creation, and the Governor Efficiency attribute. Heir designation always available, +150 Influence cost. Inheriting a higher rank doesn't change government. At peace, can ask Feudal/Clan/Theocracy(non-heads-of-faith)/Republic vassals to adopt the same government. House heads can request Gold, Scheme Success Chance, or courtiers from landed house members (more options via domicile buildings/upgrades).

If both liege and vassal have centralized-bureaucracy government: vassal can't use Independence CB, can't become a powerful vassal, can only be given titles that are de jure part of their primary title, and (if landed) can always be given directives. If the vassal's government allows title men-at-arms, the top liege can seize or reassign them for 150 Influence (scaled up for larger armies, −25% in a defensive war, −50% if the vassal is the war target). Vassals also get a distinct Petition the Liege decision menu, and can't create titles beyond Duchies or Dissolution factions, can't have a royal court, and don't use Legitimacy.

### Noble families

Any house whose head has this government is a Noble Family, with a Family Attribute and a titular County for the house head. If the top liege is centralized-bureaucracy, the top 5 Noble Families (by Powerful Family Rating, min. 40) are Powerful Families — can run Depose/Subsume Governorship political schemes with boosted success. A Powerful Family controlling ≥25% of the realm becomes Dominant (further scheme boost).

Powerful Family Rating bonuses (additive): +100 if house head is Ceremonial Monarch; +15 for 3rd Bureaucracy or 4th Virtues dynasty legacy (each); +10 each for: domicile in realm capital, Heart of the Family perk, a member with Imperial Administration type, house head is a movement leader, a member holds the Favored Minister court position; +15 per house member on the top liege's council; +10 per house in the same house bloc; +5 per house head Level of Influence (Administrative/Ritsuryō) or per Merit Rank (Celestial/Meritocratic/Meritocratic Khanate); +3 per member (non-Ritsuryō) or +1.5 per member (Ritsuryō); +1 per member holding a governorship.

### Ceremonial Monarchy

Available to Empire-rank+ realms with this government; affects all centralized-bureaucracy subjects. Adds 3 extra possible factions; title succession laws can't be used. Empire of Japan always starts with one; Unified Kingdom of Goryeo does too in the 1178 start. New ones can only form via an accepted/won Liberty faction ultimatum in a Meritocratic realm.

- **Regent Ruler**: owns the primary title, actually rules, succession law locked to Regency Succession.
- **Ceremonial Monarch**: owns a titular Kingdom, is the Regent Ruler's vassal, can't be granted/revoked titles or knighted; house Powerful Family Rating is greatly boosted; marriage into it strongly helps the liege's primary-title succession score. Regent Ruler can Force Abdication on them; Challenge Status and Raid Estate schemes can't target them.

If the same character becomes both, succession law auto-switches to Primogeniture (Japan additionally requires the Restore Direct Imperial Rule decision). Removed if the realm is conquered by an equal/higher-rank ruler, or the Ceremonial Monarch inherits a title equal/higher than the Regent Ruler's. Neither monarch can hold Coronation activities unless they're the same character.

## Governors

Duchy/Kingdom titles within an Administrative realm show in the Administrative Government tab; if their holder is also Administrative, the title is called Theme (Duchy) or Catepanate (Kingdom) and its holder becomes a Governor (gains Governor trait, provides taxes/levies scaled by Governor Efficiency). Underage vassals can't be governors. The liege can force a governor to abdicate for 150 Influence.

Governors periodically get map-based governance issues (3 reward tiers, higher tiers more likely with more Governor trait XP); solving requires traveling to the barony (unless it's the capital) and choosing an option, rewarding Gold/Prestige/Piety/Influence/trait XP; failing has minor penalties.
