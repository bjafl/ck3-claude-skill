CK3 requires learning how to manage a dynasty, not just a country. This guide covers character selection, pre-game setup, early management decisions, and succession planning to help new players avoid common pitfalls and establish a solid foundation for long-term play.

## Selecting a character

In CK3, you lead a dynasty through multiple rulers rather than controlling a single realm permanently. Choose from bookmarked characters in 867 or 1066, or click "play as any ruler" to select from any eligible ruler on the map.

For beginners, the 1066 "Rags to Riches" bookmark offers strong starting options:

- **Petty King Murchad of Munster** (Ireland): A small but defensible realm in southern Ireland. Ireland's isolation and autonomous church provide protection while you learn conquest mechanics. The tutorial assigns this character by default.
- **Duke Vratislav of Bohemia** (Holy Roman Empire): A vassal of the HRE with room to grow. Bohemia uses seniority succession (reducing divisions on death) and HRE protection. Central location teaches de jure drift mechanics.
- **Duke Robert of Apulia** (Italy): An independent ruler in southern Italy with expansion room. However, Apulia is de jure part of the Byzantine Empire, which is temporarily distracted but will eventually intervene.

Starting as a large king can be tempting but usually results in managing dozens of potentially disloyal vassals. Instead, consider playing as a count or duke and working upward. Vassals gain protection from their liege but remain vulnerable to attacks from peers and threats from the liege itself.

Before starting, adjust game rules if desired (difficulty, disease rates, heresy rates, cultural shifts, exclaves, diplomatic range). Leave these unchanged for your first game.

## Before unpausing

Before unpausing the game, handle critical tasks (indicated by diamond icons at the top of the screen):

### Choosing a lifestyle

At age 16, rulers choose one of five lifestyles (Diplomacy, Martial, Stewardship, Intrigue, Learning), each with three lifestyle focuses. Your choice grants immediate bonuses and unlocks further benefits as you progress. Pick a lifestyle matching your character's education if possible, but prioritize what your realm needs. Your education trait grants experience bonuses in its related lifestyle, but this doesn't lock you into that path.

### Marriage

Many starting rulers are unmarried. Finding a spouse before unpausing is critical, as desirable matches marry off quickly. Marriages secure alliances and claims. Consider:

- **Congenital traits** can pass to children and may be beneficial or detrimental. Inbreeding (marrying close relatives) risks undesirable traits.
- **Fertility** depends on age; females become infertile after age 45.
- **Stewardship**: Every five points of stewardship increases a character's domain limit by 1, making a spouse's stewardship valuable.
- **Matrilineal marriages** result in children of the wife's dynasty, affecting court location and inheritance. As of version 1.0.3, female AI rulers won't accept matrilineal marriages, making female-led games harder.

To arrange a marriage, click the wedding rings icon next to an unmarried character, or right-click and select Diplomacy > Find Spouse / Arrange Marriage. Use hooks or gifts to persuade liege lords if needed.

### Educating children

Children develop traits matching their guardians, especially their education trait. When you educate a child, you direct trait development but may produce stress if your choices conflict with your character's personality.

### Managing your titles

Always fully control the duchy containing your Realm Capital to consolidate power and avoid vassal resentment. If you hold counties de jure belonging to vassals' titles, plan to dispose of them eventually. Count-level rulers with multiple titles face partition succession, dividing titles among heirs. Reach duchy rank or higher to escape this, or adopt different succession laws if available.

### Foreign affairs

Survey nearby rulers for claims on your titles. Check for marriage alliance opportunities. Vassals should monitor their liege's strength and be ready to defend if attacked. If a vassal's liege is weakening, prepare to defend allies.

### Status of innovations

In 867, many cultures lack key innovations (Casus Belli for de jure claims, Plenary Assemblies for Limited Crown Authority). Decide if you want to become your culture's Head and direct innovation research. Otherwise, increase county development in your culture's lands to accelerate discovery.

## Early game

Adjust game speed after unpausing (default speed 1 is slow; speed 5 runs at maximum simulation speed). Manage both domestic and foreign affairs:

### Domestic affairs

Ensure your Realm Priest (if your faith allows) endorses you for tax and levy bonuses. Adjust your council composition to represent powerful vassals and reduce faction risk. Review your laws and succession arrangements.

#### Feudal Contracts

If feudal, review vassal obligations. Try increasing tax contributions without incurring tyranny by reducing levy contributions or granting privileges. Feudal contract changes lock in for each character's lifetime and only reset on succession.

#### Becoming Rightful Liege

Becoming your vassals' Rightful Liege maximizes tax and levy income. A non-Rightful Liege position cuts taxes and levies in half at worst. Create duchies within your de jure kingdom/empire and grant them to counts (not dukes) who aren't your de jure vassals.

### Deal with Factions as Liege

**Remove Faction Members:**
- Obtain Strong Hooks (unreliable spymaster espionage; more reliable hook fabrication with Truth is Relative perk)
- Create alliances through negotiation or marriage
- Become friends or lovers
- Increase opinion above +80 (sway, gifts)
- Transfer disloyal 2-tier or lower vassals to loyal higher-tier vassals
- Make faction members Court Chaplain if your faith allows (they can still join claimant factions)

**Push Back Discontent:**
- Increase Total Soldiers (boost Martial, hire mercenaries, recruit holy orders)
- Hard Rule perk (raises discontent threshold)
- Remove other weaker factions (each additional faction lowers discontent threshold by 5)

**Other Methods:**
- Raise Dread (less effective on bold characters)
- Assassinate claimant faction leaders
- As a vassal, petition liege for help

### Use Factions as Vassal

**When to use factions:**
- Faction members fight together against liege
- Join strong claimant factions to gain weak hooks on new lieges (useful for modifying vassal contracts, though fabricating hooks on liege may be safer/faster)
- Usurp liege titles as the claimant (create claims with Meritocracy perk)
- Gain independence with vassal support
- Destroy liege's main title to prevent future reconquest

**When not to use factions:**
- Liege is surrounded by hostile/evil faith realms; independence causes more harm than good
- Liege is not your Rightful Liege and contract is Low Obligations (you pay minimal taxes/levies while keeping protection; de jure drift may eventually fix this)

**Vassal faction tips:**
- Obtain Strong Hooks to force other vassals into factions (may be necessary if liege completed Glory Dynasty Legacy)
- Help strong factions revolt without joining (create another faction to lower discontent threshold by 5%, since revolting vassals can't join other factions)

## Preparing for succession

Succession is a perilous moment. Prepare smoothly by:

- **Know your succession laws**: Feudal succession depends on innovations and crown authority. Clan succession is usually partition/high partition (rarely splits with only one highest-tier title; costs 1500 Piety to change to single heir). Tribal succession is locked to confederate partition.
- **Ensure your heir inherits**: Verify your player heir is also your primary heir. Change succession laws to maximize your heir's inheritance.
- **Prevent realm fragmentation**: If you have multiple heirs and multiple titles of highest rank, create a higher-tier title or destroy extra equivalent titles. Exception: Confederate Partition automatically creates de jure kingdoms not yet created, assigning them to heirs. Leverage this for dynasty building.
- **Manage succession opinion**: Characters' opinions of your ruler affect opinions of your heir. Dread doesn't transfer, so amend enemy relations or neutralize threats before death.
- **Don't panic on fragmentation**: If your realm breaks apart, you gain claims to lost titles. Plot to reclaim them over generations. Unpressed claims only inherit if you fight for them while alive. Dynastic focus means titles held by dynasty members still benefit you.
- **Protect your heir**: Marrying your heir to a powerful vassal's relative strengthens protection against factions.

## Follow-up

### Authority

- **Feudal lieges**: Lower Crown Authority to ward off liberty factions (no cooldown on decreasing; only increasing has a cooldown).
- **Feudal vassals**: You can change the feudal contract once per character. If not Rightful Liege, you already have low obligations; increase one step to secure a right. Priorities: different culture/religion = seek title revocation protection; planning expansion in level 3 crown authority realm = secure sanctioned war declaration; seeking income = secure guaranteed Council Rights and force steward position.

### Succession

- **Monitor losses**: Check "Succession" tab under "Realm" window to see which titles will be lost.
- **Exit Confederate Partition early**: It's the only succession law creating new titles on succession, almost guaranteeing fragmentation unless you restrict to de jure duchy/kingdom counties. Tribal realms are locked to it and must reform to Clan or Feudal. Feudal rulers use title succession laws on primary titles to prevent fractures (must not have other titles of same rank). Alternatively, leverage Confederate Partition for Renown gain by giving junior heirs sufficient counties to avoid faction losses.
- **Manage multiple heirs**: Remove heirs if possible (force vows/monasticism after age 10; low-prowess sons serve as knights and may die; as dynasty head, disinherit all but one for Renown cost; use Sadistic trait for murder schemes). Elective title succession only applies to that title; switching Scotland to Tanistry Elective won't affect duchies/counties below it. If Scotland is your only kingdom under tanistry, you'll lose it unless your tanist is also your primary heir. Tanistry on multiple elective duchies can prevent partition splits.

### Dynasty

- **Land dynastic members**: Providing titles lets them maintain courts and procreate. Under partition, giving junior heirs titles reduces claims and infighting.
- **Allow independence**: Dynastic vassals of other dynastic members don't generate Renown. Free them to generate Renown.
- **Marry dynastic members to non-vassal rulers**: Generates 80% of the Renown from holding their primary title.
- **Prioritize dynasty interests**: Help dynastic members in wars, especially those preventing rank demotion. Disgruntled dynastic vassals can be freed or granted to liege as their vassal to avoid faction power increases while boosting Renown. Avoid dynastic wars.
