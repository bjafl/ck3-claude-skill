The Ruler Designer is a free feature added in Patch 1.2 that lets you replace any ruler in any start date (867, 1066, or 1178) with a fully customized character. Access it by selecting a historical ruler and choosing "Create your own Ruler" at game start. You can fine-tune attributes, traits, dynasty, faith, culture, and physical appearance within customization points—exceeding 400 points disables achievements.

## Essential Characteristics

The left panel sets mandatory characteristics with no customization point cost:

**Sex and Sexual Orientation**: Choose or randomly generate based on game rules.

**Faith and Culture**: No restrictions; you can select extinct religions (like Hellenism) or inactive cultures. Note: your counties won't automatically convert to your chosen faith or culture. If you pick an inactive culture, you become the Cultural head only after converting at least one county.

**Character Name**: Write your own or randomize by culture or faith.

**Dynasty and Realm**: Generate or design a dynasty name and coat of arms for both your dynasty and primary title.

## Attributes

The right panel controls character attributes, each assigned a cost in customization points (positive costs points, negative refunds them). Your total displays in the upper panel.

### Age and Weight

**Age**: Set from 0 to 120. Prime ruler age (24–28) costs the most; the youngest (0–16) and oldest (70+) cost the least. Starting age determines starting health:

| Age Range | Health | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 0–47 | Good | 5.0 |
| 48–64 | Fine | 4.0 |
| 65–120 | Fine | 3.0 |

Starting at age 15, rulers gain 1 lifestyle perk for every 3 years (1 at 18, 2 at 21, etc.), up to 20 at age 75. For every 9 perk points, you also gain a bonus lifestyle trait chosen randomly from the three trees matching your Education.

**Weight**: Range from −100 to +100. Costs no customization points but significantly affects health. Malnourished (below −50) or obese (over 50) incurs −1 Health. Noble characters naturally gain weight; starting slightly underweight (0 to −50) without hitting the malnourishment penalty is an effective strategy.

### Traits

You can choose any available traits classified as education, personality, or other. Most restrictions don't apply here—you can start with multiple personality traits (instead of the usual three), get lifestyle traits without completing their perk trees, or have traits your faith considers sins. Traits have customization point costs listed on the Traits page; if a trait has both congenial and non-congenial versions, only the congenial version is available. Virtue traits often provide more benefit than their cost implies.

### Skills

Set each of the six skills from 0 to 100. Customization point cost increases with skill threshold (terrible, poor, etc.) after the increase but before traits. For example, raising Diplomacy from 3 to 4 (crossing into "terrible") costs 2 points; from 4 to 5 (entering "poor") costs 4 points. The most cost-effective values are 1 point below threshold changes; starting with one of these and boosting further with traits is recommended.

Prowess costs half as much as other core skills and also determines cosmetic muscle mass (maxes at prowess 25).

### Family

Randomly generate family members for adult rulers with no customization cost for starting married. You can add sons and daughters (matching your faith and culture), who inherit your DNA and congenial traits but are otherwise randomized. Each child costs 10 customization points regardless of gender. A ruler can start with at most one child for each year since turning 16—a 50-year-old can have up to 34 children.

## Appearance

### Basic Customization

The first screen offers quick appearance generation. Select a predefined ethnicity and randomly generate a starting look, useful as a foundation before refinement.

This screen provides access to your character's **DNA**, a text string representing all genetic values. You can copy it to share with others, retain it for later use, or paste valid DNA strings to generate characters with matching appearances. Note: DNA only affects physical appearance; skills, attributes, lifestyle, and education must be applied separately.

### Further Customization

The second screen lets you manipulate most gene values via a graphical interface similar to standard character creators. Adjustments are purely cosmetic and don't confer mechanical effects (raising height to maximum doesn't grant the Giant trait; only the trait provides mechanical benefits).

A few genes can't be manipulated here: body shape, body hair (men), bust shape (women), style of aging, and eyebrow wrinkles. These still exist on all characters but are hidden in the interface. You can edit them by copying DNA to a text editor, changing values directly, and pasting the modified DNA back.

## Notes

After customizing a ruler, select another on the map to repeat the process. However, creating two or more custom rulers disables achievements to prevent engineering easier achievement runs. Before Patch 1.8, achievements were only disabled if you customized a ruler and then played a different historical one.
